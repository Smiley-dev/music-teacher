# 0012. Sight reading: generated melodies, played from the page

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-14

## Context

Practice needs a sight-reading exercise: a short melody in standard notation
(clef, key signature, time signature, bars, beams, rests, accidentals) that
the user can hear and then play on the instrument. The user chooses the
scales (keys), the intervals between neighbouring notes, the note lengths,
the time signature, the length and the tempo. This combines what rhythm
practice (ADR 0006, 0008) and the pitch exercises (ADR 0011) already do.

## Decision

- **Generator in `crates/theory` (`reading.rs`)**, which now depends on
  `crates/rhythm` (both stay pure and tested). The rhythm comes from
  `rhythm::generate` with the user's note values; pitches walk over the
  chosen scale inside the instrument's reading range (the staff, or up to
  the instrument's range with ledger lines). The melody starts on the tonic,
  ends on it when an allowed interval reaches it, and every step is one of
  the chosen **interval sizes**, counted in letters (unison = repeated note,
  2nd, 3rd … octave) as sight-reading method books grade them, so a "3rd" is
  major or minor as the scale has it. Each selected scale is equally likely;
  the root is any key whose signature has at most the chosen number of
  sharps or flats.
- **Key signatures** (`key.rs`): a scale is written with its parent major's
  signature (D dorian → no sharps, E minor → one sharp). Harmonic and
  melodic minor use the natural minor's signature; their raised notes show
  as accidentals.
- **`ReadingExercise`** (rhythm, clef, key signature, scale, root, and one
  written/sounding pitch per played note) is generated by the engine from
  the saved settings, rendered by the UI and sent back to start a session,
  like rhythm exercises; the engine validates it (`ReadingExercise::validate`).
- **Notation**: VexFlow (ADR 0007), one stave per bar with clef and key
  signature at the start of each line. Which notes need an accidental is
  left to `Accidental.applyAccidentals` per bar relative to the key, so the
  standard rules (accidentals last to the bar line, naturals to cancel the
  key) are followed without our own engraving logic.
- **Listen** reuses the rhythm session's listen mode (count-in, metronome,
  tones), generalised from one frequency to one frequency per note, with a
  new `SessionMode::Melody`.
- **Play at your own pace** is a `ReadingSession` using `NoteJudge` with
  exact pitch, note after note, no metronome. Because melodies may repeat a
  note, onsets re-arm the judge when the next note equals the one still
  held (`NoteJudge::onset`).
- **Play with the metronome** reuses the rhythm session in practice mode
  (timing judged as in ADR 0008) and adds a pitch check per note: the raw
  pitch readings heard while the note should sound (after a short settling
  time) vote, and the note is right if the most frequent reading is the
  written pitch. Pitch results go to the UI next to the timing judgements.
- Settings: `practice.reading` (tempo and the melody options); the
  instrument is the one shared by the pitch exercises.

## Alternatives considered

- **A separate `reading` crate** depending on `rhythm` and `theory`: one
  more crate for one module; `theory → rhythm` keeps both pure.
- **A pitch exercise task (ADR 0011) with whole notes**: no rhythm, and
  auto-advancing tasks do not fit reading a page of music.
- **Interval sizes in semitones** (minor 3rd, major 3rd …): melodies stay in
  a scale, so the scale decides the quality; letter distance is what a
  reader sees on the staff.
- **Judging pitch at the onset** in metronome mode: the pitch tracker needs
  about 80 ms of signal, so the attack alone is not enough; voting over the
  note's length is more robust to attack transients.

## Consequences

- Reading reuses the timing judge, onset detector, note judge and tone
  playback; no new audio code.
- Melodies are monophonic (no chords, no ties across bar lines, as rhythm
  exercises).
- Very short notes at fast tempos give the pitch check few readings; such
  notes are reported as "pitch unclear" rather than wrong.
